🧭 The hunter's mindset

The course’s turning point: you don’t need to be a creative genius. You need to be a good hunter—someone who finds what’s already selling, understands the formula, and makes your own version. Here, you’ll learn to think that way.

1

🎯 Don't Reinvent the Wheel

It’s extremely rare for a brand-new ad to take off on the first try. Anyone who works in advertising knows: the smart way is start from what has already proven to sell and make your own version. The wheel has already been invented — your job is to put it to work in your business.

🔑 The golden rule

Creating from scratch = an expensive, risky bet. Copying a winner's structure = lower risk and faster results. You're not "cheating"; you're being efficient.

2

🔭 Hunt, study, launch

The whole method fits into three moves. Each track in this course is one of them — keep this map handy, because it organizes everything that comes next.

1

Hunt (Track 2)

Find the ads that are already selling in the free libraries.

2

Study (Track 3)

Break down the ad and understand why it works.

3

Launch (Track 4)

Recreate with AI, make several versions, and test until you find the winner.

3

📚 The spy archive

Maybe the best news in the course: Meta’s (Facebook/Instagram) and TikTok’s ad libraries are public and free. Everything your competitors are running is there for everyone to see. It’s your official, legal “spy file.”

🔗 Where it goes

  • Meta: facebook.com/ads/library
  • TikTok: library.tiktok.com

You’ll use these two libraries extensively in Track 2. For now, just know they exist and they’re free.

4

🧪 Copy the framework, don't plagiarize

This is where the smart researcher differs from the lazy copycat. You copy the structure what works—the type of hook, the pace, the call to action—but with your product, your brand, and your images.

✓ Copy

  • ✓The hook format ("I found a trick...").
  • ✓The pacing and order of the information.
  • ✓The type of call to action.

✗ Don’t copy

  • ✗The competitor's product.
  • ✗His images and video.
  • ✗Its brand and name.
5

🔁 The winner becomes 100 versions

When a brand finds a winning ad, it doesn't stand still: it makes dozens of small variations of it. Same formula, swapped details — blue shirt, red shirt, indoors, on the street. This behavior is a golden clue.

💡 Detective tip

Have you seen the same ad in 30 nearly identical versions? That’s not waste—it’s the brand saying, “this formula is making me money.” Write down that formula.

6

⚖️ Only advertise what already has traction

A principle that saves a lot of money: it’s worth scaling with ads what already shows signs of life — sells organically or already works in other people’s ads. Don’t force something that’s never been tested just because you like it.

🚦 The green signal

  • Already selling on its own organically (without paying for ads)? → Green light.
  • Competitors have been running this for a long time? → Green light.
  • No one has tested it, and you just "think it’ll work"? → Yellow flag; be careful.
