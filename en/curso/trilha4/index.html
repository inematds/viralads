🤖 The AI workflow

Three tools working together: one that thinks and coordinates, one that generates the video, and a platform that brings it all together. You provide the template and get the ad.

What it is:

An AI “conductor” (like Claude Code) that receives your request, analyzes the reference video, and gives instructions to the other tools.

Why learn:

It’s what turns “I want an ad like this” into concrete steps, without you having to do everything by hand.

Key concepts:

Coordinates · analyzes · gives orders. The brain of the workflow.

What it is:

The tool (such as Seedance 2.0) that actually creates the UGC-style video from an image and instructions.

Why learn:

It’s what works the “miracle” of turning a product photo into a video of someone talking.

Key concepts:

Image + instruction → video. The production arm.

What it is:

A platform (like Arcads) with a library of actors, voices, and everything ready to go, where you build the ad without code.

Why learn:

It’s the easiest way for people who don’t code: you drag, choose, and generate.

Key concepts:

Ready-made actors + voices · no code · simple interface.

What it is:

The complete sequence: you provide a sample video, AI analyzes it, writes a script in the same style for your product, and generates the video.

Why learn:

It’s the heart of Track 4. Understanding the workflow puts you in control of every step.

Key concepts:

Model → analysis → script → video. Four steps.

What it is:

You can also take a video that works and ask: "replace the product with this one" and "replace the actor with this other one".

Why learn:

It’s the fastest way to adapt a winner to your product while keeping the winning formula.

Key concepts:

Same structure, your product, your actor. Smart adaptation.

What it is:

You provide the Track 3 template + a photo of your product, and the AI writes your ad script in that style.

Why learn:

It’s the bridge between studying (Track 3) and the finished video (this module).

Key concepts:

Template + product photo → finished script.

🎙️ Actor and Voice: The Voice Is Everything

The golden rule of UGC: people forgive bad visuals, but they never forgive a bad voice. Learn to choose the right actor and engineer a voice that sounds human.

What it is:

Platforms have libraries of AI “actors.” You search by gender, age, and type, then choose who will speak.

Why learn:

The right actor creates an immediate connection with the viewer.

Key concepts:

Search by gender/age · young, adult, senior · choose and you’re done.

What it is:

Choose an actor who resembles your ideal customer — the same age range, the same "world."

Why learn:

We trust people who are like us more. Different actors speak to different audiences.

Key concepts:

Mirror your customer · senior audiences respond to seniors.

What it is:

The most important takeaway from the learning path: the voice carries trust. People forgive poor visuals, but they don't forgive a bad voice.

Why learn:

If you can only perfect one thing, perfect the voice.

Key concepts:

Voice = trust. Invest time here.

What it is:

Instead of having AI "read" a text, you record yourself speaking, and the tool copies your cadence and delivers it in the avatar's voice.

Why learn:

A voice copied from real people sounds much more natural than a voice "reading."

Key concepts:

Record your speech · AI copies the cadence · human result.

What it is:

Two controls: stability (more steady vs. more expressive) and similarity (how much the voice resembles your recording).

Why learn:

Adjusting these settings is what fine-tunes the voice until it sounds truly natural.

Key concepts:

Stability = consistency. Similarity = resemblance. Test both.

What it is:

Before generating the entire video, listen to the voice preview and adjust the speed and controls.

Why learn:

Fixing the voice in the preview saves time and prevents you from generating a video with a bad voice.

Key concepts:

Listen · adjust · only then generate the video.

🛡️ Realism, variations, and security

The details that separate an ad that sells from one that looks fake—and how to test several versions and protect your passwords along the way.

What it is:

For UGC, don’t use the highest resolution possible. Everyday people don’t record in perfect 4K—that gives away that it’s an ad.

Why learn:

A little imperfection builds trust. Realism sells better than perfection.

Key concepts:

Avoid 4K · make it look homemade · imperfections = authenticity.

What it is:

Generate multiple versions of the same ad (different hooks, actors, and voices) and run them all to see which sells more.

Why learn:

It’s the same secret as Track 1: the winner emerges when you test several variations.

Key concepts:

Many versions · let the audience choose · scale the winner.

What it is:

Set up the video in the right format: vertical (9:16) for feed/stories and short (around 10-15 seconds).

Why learn:

The wrong format kills reach. Vertical and short is what the feed rewards.

Key concepts:

9:16 · short and direct · designed for mobile.

What it is:

The tools use “API keys” (an access password). Never paste that password directly into an AI conversation.

Why learn:

Ask the AI for a command that stores the password securely, outside the conversation history.

Key concepts:

Key = password · never in chat · use a secure command.

What it is:

Platforms charge by credit or monthly plan. You can start small and grow as results come in.

Why learn:

Knowing the cost helps you avoid surprises and calculate whether the ad is making a profit.

Key concepts:

Credits or monthly subscription · start small · measure the return.

What it is:

Run your variations, see which one sells, put more money behind the winner, and repeat the process.

Why learn:

It’s how the whole method pays for itself: you only scale what you’ve already proven works.

Key concepts:

Test → measure → scale the winner → repeat.
