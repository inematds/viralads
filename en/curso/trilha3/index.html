🔬 The 5 dimensions of analysis

Every ad that sells can be broken down into five parts. Learn to look at each one, and you’ll never watch an ad the same way again.

What it is:

The first line or image that grabs attention. It’s what makes someone stop scrolling the feed.

Why learn:

Without a hook, no one watches the rest. It's the most important part of the entire ad.

Key concepts:

"I found a trick..." · a question · a surprising result.

What it is:

The pace at which scenes and lines follow one another throughout the video.

Why learn:

The right pace keeps people watching until the end. Quick cuts hold their attention; too much slowness loses them.

Key concepts:

Frequent cuts · no dead time · pick up the pace at the beginning.

What it is:

The speaker’s voice and the background music. In UGC, the voice is what builds (or destroys) trust the most.

Why learn:

People forgive poor image quality, but they don’t forgive a bad voice. It’s a pillar of the ad.

Key concepts:

Natural voice · current music sound · conversational cadence.

What it is:

The structure: how the video introduces the problem, shows the solution, and leads to the offer.

Why learn:

The right order naturally leads people to want to buy.

Key concepts:

Problem → solution → proof → offer. Always in that order.

What it is:

CTA is the “call to action”—the “buy now,” “order today,” “click the link.” And where it appears matters.

Why learn:

Without a clear call to action, people may like the video but won't act. The CTA closes the sale.

Key concepts:

Direct · just one action · repeated at the end.

What it is:

A small card where you note the 5 dimensions for each ad: hook, pacing, audio, narrative, CTA.

Why learn:

Taking notes turns "guesswork" into a pattern. After several worksheets, what repeats becomes your formula.

Key concepts:

Use the same worksheet for everyone. Compare. Look for what repeats.

🎞️ Frame-by-Frame Analysis and Transcription

How to watch an ad in slow motion—on-screen text, shots, visuals—and how to let AI do the heavy lifting for you.

What it is:

Look at the ad scene by scene, almost pausing the video, to see every decision that was made.

Why learn:

At normal speed, you just "get a feel for it." Frame by frame, you understand exactly what they did.

Key concepts:

Pause · rewind · notice the details that are easy to miss.

What it is:

The words that appear on the video—captions, highlights, price.

Why learn:

Many people watch without sound. On-screen text is what communicates with them.

Key concepts:

Always use captions · highlight the keyword · show the price.

What it is:

Each “shot” is one piece of a scene: a person speaking, a product in close-up, hands using it.

Why learn:

The right sequence of shots shows the product in a way that makes people want it.

Key concepts:

Face · product close-up · result. Vary the shots.

What it is:

The video's overall "look": colors, lighting, setting (home, street, kitchen).

Why learn:

The right visual style reinforces the UGC feeling that "this is real, it's an everyday person."

Key concepts:

Home setting · natural light · no perfect studio.

What it is:

Transcribe everything the person says in the video—the script, word for word.

Why learn:

With the script in front of you, you can see the structure of the speech and adapt it for your product.

Key concepts:

AI transcribes it automatically. You get a ready-to-study script.

What it is:

You give the AI the video, and it breaks everything down: scene by scene, on-screen text, transcript, and why it works.

Why learn:

What would take an hour to do by hand, AI does in minutes — and in more detail.

Key concepts:

Video goes in · full analysis comes out. You decide what to use.

🗂️ Catalog and find the patterns

One ad is an anecdote. Twenty ads reveal a pattern. Learn to catalog them and extract the winning template in your niche.

What it is:

Gather 15 to 20 winning ads from the same niche and analyze them all using the same worksheet.

Why learn:

With enough examples, coincidences stop being luck and become the niche’s rule.

Key concepts:

15-20 ads · same niche · same worksheet.

What it is:

Count how many times each type of hook, pacing, or CTA appears in your collection.

Why learn:

What appears in 15 out of 20 ads is almost certainly what works.

Key concepts:

Count. The most frequent is the most likely winner.

What it is:

The things winning ads have in common—the “style” the whole niche uses.

Why learn:

These are the commonalities you’ll copy in your own ad.

Key concepts:

What's common among winners = the safe path.

What it is:

For each ad, separate 5 pieces: the hook, the primary text, the headline, the call to action, and the landing page.

Why learn:

Separately, these pieces become a menu you can mix and match to build your ad.

Key concepts:

Hook · copy · headline · CTA · landing page. The 5 pieces of the puzzle.

What it is:

A model script for your niche, built from the common patterns you found.

Why learn:

This template is what you'll give the AI in Track 4 to make your video.

Key concepts:

A reusable template. Just swap the product and brand.

What it is:

The more you analyze and run (50 to 100), the clearer and more reliable the niche's pattern becomes.

Why learn:

It’s work that builds over time: each ad you analyze makes you sharper.

Key concepts:

Volume = clarity. The pattern rewards patience.
